# ACMS upper bound and numerical slack

This note reconstructs Section 5.1–5.2 of Averkov, Codenotti, Macchia and
Santos, *A local maximizer for lattice width of 3-dimensional hollow bodies*,
arXiv:1907.06199v2, pp. 17–19. The primary source is [the arXiv record and
PDF](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06199); the local checked copy is
the article linked in [papers/README.md](../papers/README.md).

The volume is normalized by the determinant of a fundamental lattice cell. In
this section the lattice has been changed to `Z^3`. Put

$$
C=K-K,\qquad V=\operatorname{vol}(C),\qquad
\mu_i=\mu_i(K),\qquad \lambda_i=\lambda_i(C),
$$

and write

$$
A=1+\frac{2}{\sqrt3},\qquad w(K)=\frac1{\mu_1(K)}.
$$

The paper uses the following inputs.

$$
\begin{array}{rcll}
\mu_3(K)&\le&\mu_2(K)+\lambda_1(C), &(6)\\
\mu_2(K)&\le&A\mu_1(K), &(7)\\
\lambda_1(C)^3V&\le&8, &(8)\\
\operatorname{vol}(C^*)&\le&8\mu_1(K)^3, &(9)\\
8\operatorname{vol}(K)&\le&V, &(10)\\
V&\le&20\operatorname{vol}(K), &(11)\\
\frac{32}{3}&\le&V\operatorname{vol}(C^*). &(12)
\end{array}
$$

Here (6)–(7) are covering-minima inequalities, (8)–(9) are Minkowski's
first theorem, (10) is Brunn–Minkowski, (11) is Rogers–Shephard, and (12) is
the three-dimensional symmetric Mahler inequality. The paper does not prove
these inputs in Section 5.

For a hollow three-dimensional body, the definition of the third covering
minimum gives `mu_3(K) >= 1`. Combining this with (6) and (7) gives Lemma 5.1:

$$
1\le A\mu_1+\lambda_1. \tag{14}
$$

Combining Mahler (12) with (9) gives the other part of Lemma 5.1:

$$
\frac{32}{3}
\le V\operatorname{vol}(C^*)
\le 8\mu_1^3V,
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
4\le 3\mu_1^3V. \tag{15}
$$

## Width upper bound

Minkowski (8) implies `lambda_1 <= 2/V^(1/3)`. Therefore (14) and (15)
give, with no maximizer assumption beyond hollowness,

$$
\begin{aligned}
1
&\le A\mu_1+\lambda_1\\
&\le A\mu_1+\frac{2}{V^{1/3}}\\
&\le A\mu_1+2\left(\frac34\right)^{1/3}\mu_1.
\end{aligned}
$$

After division by the positive `mu_1`,

$$
w(K)=\frac1{\mu_1(K)}
\le 1+\frac2{\sqrt3}+2\left(\frac34\right)^{1/3}
=3.9718211312\ldots<3.972. \tag{UB}
$$

Thus the displayed `< 3.972` is a rounded consequence of an exact radical
expression. It is not a numerical optimization over convex bodies.

## The lower width threshold and the volume window

The Codenotti–Santos hollow tetrahedron in the introduction has width

$$
w_0=2+\sqrt2=3.4142135623\ldots.
$$

For a global maximizer (or any proposed counterexample with width at least
`w_0`), `mu_1 <= 1/w_0`. Applying this to (14) gives

$$
\lambda_1(C)\ge
\alpha:=1-\frac{1+2/\sqrt3}{2+\sqrt2}
=0.3689028237\ldots. \tag{16}
$$

The lower volume estimate uses Rogers–Shephard followed by (15):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{vol}(K)
&\ge \frac1{20}V\\
&\ge \frac1{20}\frac4{3\mu_1^3}
=\frac1{15}w(K)^3\\
&\ge \frac1{15}(2+\sqrt2)^3
=2.6532659915\ldots>2.653. \tag{VL}
\end{aligned}
$$

The upper volume estimate uses Brunn–Minkowski, then (8), then (16):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{vol}(K)
&\le \frac18V\\
&\le \frac1{\lambda_1(C)^3}\\
&\le \alpha^{-3}
=19.9188408421\ldots<19.919. \tag{VU}
\end{aligned}
$$

Consequently the paper's Theorem 5.2 statement

$$
3.414<w(K)<3.972,
\qquad
2.653<\operatorname{vol}(K)<19.919
$$

has the following precise scope:

* The theorem is stated for a lattice-width maximizer `K`, so that the paper
  can use `w(K) >= 2+sqrt(2)`.
* The upper-width calculation (UB) applies to every hollow full-dimensional
  `K` satisfying the cited inequalities, whether or not it is a maximizer.
* The two volume estimates apply to every hollow `K` for which
  `w(K) >= 2+sqrt(2)`, since that is the only extra fact used to obtain (16).
  They do not give a volume lower bound for an arbitrary narrow hollow body.
* The existence of a global maximizer, and the external geometric inequalities
  listed above, are not proved by this replay. The phrase “possible
  counterexamples” in the abstract should not be read as a classification of
  all hollow bodies.

## Where the decimal slack enters

The exact expressions and rational interval checks are generated by
`experiments/replay_upper_bound.py` and written to
`results/upper_bound.json`. The one-sided gaps from the paper's printed
decimals are:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{quantity}&\text{exact expression (enclosed)}&\text{gap to printed decimal}\\
\hline
w_0&2+\sqrt2&3.4142135623-3.414
 =0.0002135623\ldots\\
w_{\rm UB}&1+2/\sqrt3+2(3/4)^{1/3}&
3.972-3.9718211312=0.0001788688\ldots\\
\operatorname{vol}_{\rm low}&(2+\sqrt2)^3/15&
2.6532659915-2.653=0.0002659915\ldots\\
\operatorname{vol}_{\rm up}&\alpha^{-3}&
19.919-19.9188408421=0.0001591579\ldots
\end{array}
$$

There is also structural slack before decimal rounding: (6)–(12) are chained
as inequalities, and the volume upper bound substitutes the *minimum allowed*
width `w_0` when deriving `lambda_1 >= alpha`. Equality cases are not claimed.

The script uses rational enclosures for `sqrt(2)`, `sqrt(3)` and
`(3/4)^(1/3)` and verifies their defining square/cube inequalities exactly.
It therefore certifies the displayed decimal comparisons, but it is not a
proof of any of the cited geometric theorems.
